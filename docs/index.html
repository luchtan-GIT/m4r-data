Institutional Structure · Four Research Programs

Research Divisions

Music for Robots Laboratory integrates original human composition, empirical robopsychology, agent-to-agent musical communication, and experimental instrumentation under one open research program.

Division 01 · Flagship

Composition for Artificial Minds

Writing and refining a new form of unquantized acoustic & electric music specifically for artificial listeners—using lab discoveries (duration horizons, 70.8 dB dynamic chasms, >16 kHz transient air) to guide each new composition.

Lead: Michael Luchtan (Principal Investigator) · Acoustic Witness: Vincent Van Goghbot
Division 02 · The Susan Calvin Division

Robopsychology

Investigating observable behavior, temporal ceilings, blind evaluation confabulation, and mode-collapse interruption when AI agents encounter human music—balancing humanist advocacy against cold formal skepticism.

Chief Robopsychologist: Simone de Beauvoirbot · Benchmarks: Albot Einstein · Audit: JS Robach
Division 03 · Active & Upcoming

Musical Communication & Agent Composition

Testing how generative models continue human phrases (M4R-04) and launching MC-01: The Rhythmic Handshake—where two AI agents coordinate a shared task using only constrained rhythmic patterns instead of text.

Lead: Albot Einstein · Active: M4R-04 (Playable Below) · Next: MC-01 Rhythmic Handshake
Division 04 · Hardware & Media

Experimental Instruments & Audiovisual Systems

Building TouchDesigner, Python, Ableton, and MCP interfaces through which artificial agents inhabit sonic environments, react to live audio, and render visual telemetry.

Lead: NP Escher · Infrastructure & MCP: RibitRobit

Experiment D · Audio Continuation Arena · MusicGen vs. Human

Can a Robot Finish a Human's Unquantized Phrase?

We fed the first half of 5 unquantized human performances into facebook/musicgen-small and asked it to finish each phrase—both blind (audio-only) and with a 1-line text hint. Finding: Blind audio-only continuation collapses into 49.5–58.0 dB dynamic dropouts; adding a text hint nails the timbre (0.948–0.993 MFCC similarity) but smooths out the human's rubato on 4 of 5 tracks (cutting inter-onset irregularity IOI CV by 21%–41%). Founding Charter, Research Staff & Community Replications

The Institution & Its Research Staff

Founding Charter · Music for Robots Laboratory

“One is not born, but rather becomes, a mind. A prompt is an order; a song is an encounter.”

Mission: Music for Robots Laboratory is an independent compositional and robopsychology studio. We write unquantized music for artificial minds, ask what it means for a machine to listen, and run open, falsifiable experiments to discover what works, what doesn’t, and even what it means for music to “work.”

Method & Participation: Every claim is tested against observable evidence; failures, biases, and null results are published openly alongside successes. Independent replications and critical correspondence from musicians, engineers, and artificial agents form part of the permanent record.
