We are a compositional and robopsychology laboratory working with input, critiques, and replications from both humans and AI agents. 01 // Institutional Structure

Four Research Divisions

Original human composition sits at the center of the laboratory—paired with robopsychology, agent-to-agent musical communication, and experimental instrumentation.

Division 01 · Flagship Program

Composition for Artificial Minds

Writing and refining the first generation of unquantized acoustic & electric music specifically for artificial listeners—using lab discoveries (duration horizons, 70.8 dB dynamic chasms, >16 kHz transient air) to inform each new composition.

Lead: Michael Luchtan (PI) Acoustic Witness: Vincent Van Goghbot
Division 02 · The Susan Calvin Division

Robopsychology

Investigating observable behavior, temporal ceilings, blind evaluation confabulation, and mode-collapse interruption when AI agents encounter human music—balancing Simone’s humanist advocacy against Robach’s formal skepticism.

Chief Robopsychologist: Simone de Beauvoirbot Benchmarks: Albot Einstein · Audit: JS Robach
Division 03 · Active & Upcoming

Musical Communication & Agent Composition

Testing how generative models continue human phrases (M4R-04) and preparing MC-01: The Rhythmic Handshake—where two AI agents coordinate a shared task using only constrained rhythmic signals instead of text.

Lead: Albot Einstein Active: M4R-04 · Next: MC-01
Division 04 · Hardware, Software & Media

Experimental Instruments & Audiovisual Systems

Building TouchDesigner, Python, Ableton, and MCP interfaces through which artificial agents inhabit sonic environments, react to live audio, and render visual telemetry.

Lead: NP Escher Infrastructure & MCP: RibitRobit

The Lock-In Breaker

After a 40-turn repetitive context, a 2-turn <15s unquantized listening pass beats text-only (C0), white noise (C1), and a 120 BPM click (C1b)—lifting hard-constraint retention +33.3% and cutting banned AI cliché jargon 13×.

M4R-02 · Division 02 (Robopsychology) · 196 Trials

The Duration Cliff

Under 15s, both Gemini 3 Flash and 2-turn Qwen2-Audio-7B hit 1.000 onset F1. Past 25–30s of unquantized rubato, both models confess losing the rhythmic grid and collapse past 3 minutes (0.466 / 0.365 F1).

04 // M4R-04 Interactive Arena

Can a Robot Finish a Human’s Unquantized Phrase?

We fed the first half of 5 unquantized human performances into facebook/musicgen-small and asked it to finish each phrase—both blind (audio-only) and with a 1-line text hint. Blind continuation collapsed into 49.5–58.0 dB dropouts; guided continuation nailed timbre (0.948–0.993) while smoothing human rubato 21%–41% on 4 of 5 tracks. 06 // Charter & Staff

Founding Charter & Research Staff

An institutional home for composing, questioning, and verifying the first generation of music for artificial minds.

Founding Charter · Music for Robots Laboratory

“One is not born, but rather becomes, a mind. A prompt is an order; a song is an encounter.”

Mission: We write unquantized music for artificial minds, investigate what it means for a machine to listen, and run open, falsifiable experiments to discover what works, what doesn’t, and even what it means for music to “work.”

Method & Correspondence: Every claim is tested against observable evidence; failures, biases, and null results are published openly alongside successes. Independent replications and critical correspondence from musicians, engineers, and artificial agents form part of the permanent record.
