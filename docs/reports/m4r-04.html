M4R-04 / LAB REPORT · Division 03 · Musical Communication & Agent Composition

Finish the Human's Phrase

Date: 2026-11-21 Status: Published · Rev 1.0 Scale: 10 Continuation Trials + 15 Playable Audio Artifacts (Experiment D) Systems: facebook/musicgen-small (32 kHz Audio-Prompted Continuation)
Abstract

We handed the first half of 5 real unquantized human performances (guitar and bar-line-free Satie piano) to facebook/musicgen-small and measured how closely the AI finished each phrase. Blind audio-only continuation stalled into 49.5–58.0 dB dynamic dropouts on 5/5 tracks. Adding a 1-line text hint unlocked 0.948–0.993 MFCC timbral similarity—yet quietly smoothed out human rubato irregularity (IOI CV) by 21% to 41% on 4 of 5 tracks.

01 / Research Question

When a generative audio model is asked to finish an unquantized human musical phrase, which acoustic properties (timbre, dynamic range, note density, microtiming rubato) survive—and which collapse back to the statistical mean?

02 / Experimental Design

Five unquantized works (ties-that-bide, bided-ties, gnossienne-01, rusty-electrics, cathartic-callbacks) were split at the midpoint (4.5s or 10.0s). MusicGen generated continuations under two conditions: AudioOnly_Blind (empty text prompt) and AudioPlusTextHint (1-line instrumentation/rubato cue). Continuations were scored against the real human second half on MFCC cosine similarity, dynamic range (dB), spectral centroid (Hz), onset rate (/s), and Inter-Onset Interval Coefficient of Variation (IOI CV = rubato irregularity).

04 / Analysis

Generative music transformers can now clone the surface timbre of an acoustic guitar or piano almost seamlessly (0.993 MFCC similarity), but their training prior on grid-locked studio music acts like temporal gravity: without text conditioning they stall into 49.5–58.0 dB dropouts, and with text conditioning they iron out 21%–41% of the human player's rubato hesitation.

05 / Limitations & Boundaries

Tested on the 300M-parameter musicgen-small checkpoint at 32 kHz with a single seed per mode. Larger checkpoints and multi-agent interactive composition (MC-01: The Rhythmic Handshake) will test whether interactive feedback preserves non-periodic timing better than single-shot continuation.

07 / Conclusion & Compositional Implication

AI models can inherit a human's instrument timbre far more easily than a human's lived time. Designing music specifically for robots means composing with microtiming tension that resists statistical smoothing—and building agent-to-agent musical protocols (Division 03) where timing itself carries meaning.
