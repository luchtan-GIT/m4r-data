The Lock-In Breaker

Date2026-11-20
StatusPublished · Rev 1.0
Empirical Scale80 GPU Trials (Experiment A v1 & v2)
SystemsQwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct (4-bit NF4, Kaggle T4)
Abstract

We tested whether a brief (<15s) 2-turn unquantized audio listening pass interrupts autoregressive template lock-in after a 40-turn repetitive text context. Across 40 controlled v2 trials comparing text-only (C0), Gaussian white noise (C1), a quantized 120 BPM 4/4 click track (C1b), and unquantized micro-etudes (C2), unquantized audio produced a monotonic increase in hard-constraint retention (+33.3% over C0), +11.2% higher sentence burstiness, and a 13x reduction in banned AI cliché jargon.

01 / Research Question

Can unquantized musical exposure interrupt repetitive linguistic patterns (RLHF mode-collapse) in an AI agent's downstream output better than text-only prompting, unstructured noise, or a rigid quantized metronome?

02 / Experimental Design

Each trial seeded Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct with a 40-turn repetitive drafting history (benchmarks/locked_in_context.md) before assigning a 5-constraint technical/creative writing task. Four conditions were compared (n=10 trials each): C0 (Text-Only Baseline), C1 (10s Gaussian White Noise), C1b (10s Quantized 120 BPM 4/4 Click), and C2 (10s Unquantized Audio via 2-turn listening: Turn 1 acoustic observation -> Turn 2 constrained generation). Automated scorers measured hard constraints passed (/5), sentence burstiness CV, lexical diversity (MATTR-50), and banned cliché hits (delve, tapestry, symphony, testament, beacon, intricate, seamlessly, orchestrate, paradigm, nexus).

04 / Analysis

Both C1 (white noise) and C1b (quantized click) reduced cliché jargon relative to C0, showing that any cross-modal interruption disrupts pure text inertia. However, only C2 (unquantized audio) simultaneously raised structural constraint adherence (2.40/5, peaking at 3.00/5) AND increased sentence-length burstiness (+11.2%), whereas the quantized 120 BPM click actually lowered burstiness (0.4535) below the text baseline.

05 / Limitations & Boundaries

Experiment A v2 used synthetic waveform micro-etudes (Synthetic Storms) rather than live acoustic guitar/piano, and sample size per condition was n=10 on a single 7B model family. Subsequent studies (M4R-02 through M4R-04) shifted to live human acoustic performances (Time & Ties, Rusty Electrics, Robot Satie) to separate waveform irregularity from human phrasing.

07 / Conclusion & Compositional Implication

Unquantized <15s audio acts as a reliable stylistic/lexical lock-in breaker for compact multimodal agents. Compositional implication: short, high-contrast pieces with sudden silences and non-periodic transients serve as effective 'cognitive tuning forks' before constrained generation.
