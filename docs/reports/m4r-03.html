The Human and the Quantized Twin

Date2026-11-21
StatusPublished · Rev 1.0
Empirical Scale42 GPU Trials (Experiment C)
SystemsQwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct (4-bit NF4, Kaggle T4)
Abstract

Inspired by a methodological critique from Substack reader Martin Elisaleco, Experiment C tested three hard failure modes on Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct: (1) blind A/B discrimination between real human recordings and their own quantized twins, (2) progressive 10s–60s timing probes across Rusty Electrics and free-time Robot Satie voices, and (3) code-review bug catching under sycophantic pressure. Results exposed a 100% Clip-A position-bias confabulation illusion (8/8 trials), a hard 0–10s timestamp ceiling past 30s, and an honest null result on 7B Python debugging.

01 / Research Question

Can a 7B audio-LLM distinguish real unquantized human timing from its own quantized twin in a counterbalanced blind test? What exact mechanism drives the 30s duration cliff? And does unquantized audio improve symbolic code-bug detection?

02 / Experimental Design

Part 1 (n=8): For 4 tracks (ties-that-bide, bided-ties, gnossienne-01, rusty-electrics), we built quantized twins via librosa.onset.onset_detect + librosa.effects.time_stretch (snapping inter-onset intervals to the median grid) and presented [Clip A, Clip B] blind in both orders. Part 2 (n=10): Probed 10s/20s/30s/45s/60s slices of rusty-electrics and gnossienne-01. Part 3 (n=24): Compared C0, C1b (120 BPM click), Quantized Twin, and Human Unquantized on Python mutable-default/off-by-one bugs and Bat-and-Ball sycophancy traps.

04 / Analysis

M4R-03 establishes essential scientific guardrails for Robopsychology: (a) never trust un-counterbalanced A/B audio preference evals on 7B models, because LLM position bias overrides the audio encoder and generates convincing poetic confabulations; (b) Qwen2-Audio-7B's 30s cliff is driven by a literal 10-second timestamp coordinate ceiling; and (c) unquantized music alters stylistic entropy and lock-in—not symbolic 7B Python debugging.

05 / Limitations & Boundaries

Phase-vocoder time-stretching introduces minor spectral artifacts on dense chords, though the 100% Clip-A order bias proves the model did not exploit those artifacts. Future work (RP-01) will replicate M4R-03 on frontier and next-gen open audio models.
