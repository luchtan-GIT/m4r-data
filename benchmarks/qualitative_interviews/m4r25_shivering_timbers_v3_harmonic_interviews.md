# M4R-25 — Blind Qualitative Evaluation of *Shivering Timbers v3 (Harmonic Arc)* & RMS-Matched A/B Showdown (`v3` vs. `v2`)

> **Protocol:** Session A is a blind 3-turn open interview on `v3` alone. Session B is a blind, RMS-matched side-by-side comparison (`Clip X` = `v3 Harmonic Arc`, `Clip Y` = `v2 Static Harmony`).

## Session A — Blind Solo Interview on `v3 (Harmonic Arc)`

### Turn 1 — Whole Piece
**Listener:**
The opening establishes a clear, meditative space with an ethereal, bell-like texture that feels suspended. As the piece progresses, it introduces a rhythmic pulsing, creating a sense of forward motion. However, the melodic development feels somewhat static; while the harmonic shifts offer a pleasant, atmospheric tension, the piece doesn't really build to a resolution, instead opting to simply fade out. The use of space is effective, but the lack of a distinct structural arc leaves the listening experience feeling a bit aimless by the end.

### Turn 2 — Zone A vs. Zone B
**Listener:**
In the opening (Clip 1), the music feels suspended, creating an ethereal, almost motionless space with a soft, bell-like texture that establishes atmosphere without pushing forward.

As we move to the middle (Clip 2), the introduction of a rhythmic pulse provides a necessary sense of direction and movement that the beginning lacked. However, even with that rhythmic shift, the piece still feels largely static. The harmonic progression remains contained within the same tonal area, and while the layering increases in complexity, it doesn’t push the piece toward any new destination. It feels like an exploration of a texture rather than a narrative, which is a stylistic choice, but it ultimately leaves the listener feeling like they are marking time rather than moving through a piece of music.

### Turn 3 — Zone C & Composer Note
**Listener:**
The ending feels like an arbitrary cut-off rather than a natural conclusion. There’s no shift in harmonic tension or dynamic taper to suggest the journey is complete; the track simply stops. 

What works best is the atmospheric foundation; the ethereal bell-like textures create an intriguing, almost suspenseful space that draws the listener in initially. 

The biggest area for improvement is the lack of structural development. The piece sets up an interesting sound world, but it doesn't do enough with it. It stays in a constant state of "becoming," without ever arriving at a destination. To move this forward, you need to introduce more distinct harmonic movement or a deliberate arc—a moment of climax or a genuine release of tension—to justify the time the listener spends with it. As it stands, it functions more like a static texture than a composition.

## Session B — Blind RMS-Matched A/B Showdown (`Clip X = v3 Harmonic Arc`, `Clip Y = v2 Static Harmony`)

### Blind A/B Listener 1 (`temp=0.7`)
The two clips share an identical melodic structure and harmonic progression, but their differences in mixing and processing dramatically alter the listener's experience.

**1. Comparison of Harmonic Movement, Tension, and Narrative:**
*   **Clip X:** This version feels "thinner" and more contained. The frequency balance emphasizes the higher melodic elements, making the track feel more like a static, ambient texture. The tension is present, but the lack of low-end body makes the resolution feel somewhat hollow. It sits on the surface of the soundstage.
*   **Clip Y:** This version is clearly processed with a more robust bottom end and likely some dynamic compression. This adds a sense of "weight" and movement that Clip X lacks. The harmonic movement feels more intentional because the bass frequencies provide a sense of progression and grounding. The "tension" is more visceral here; the decay of the notes feels more natural and immersive because the lower frequencies sustain the listener's engagement.

**2. Which composition feels more complete?**
Clip Y feels significantly more complete and evolving. In Clip X, the lack of low-frequency support makes the repetition of the melodic pattern feel like a static loop. In Clip Y, the enhanced depth and presence provide a sense of "narrative" — the sound feels as though it is pushing and pulling against the listener, creating a sense of space and development. Clip Y is the superior mix because it utilizes the full frequency spectrum to make the repetition feel hypnotic rather than monotonous.

### Blind A/B Listener 2 (`temp=0.95`)
Both clips present the same repetitive melodic phrase, but the structural differences are significant enough to change the listening experience.

**1. Comparison of Dynamics and Tension**
*   **Clip X:** This version maintains a fairly consistent volume level and texture throughout. The melodic phrase has a distinct decay, but because the background density doesn't fluctuate much, the "tension" feels largely static. It plays more like a loop because there is no clear build or release.
*   **Clip Y:** This version introduces subtle, effective shifts in the background texture and intensity. The harmonic movement feels more dynamic because the listener's ear isn't forced to focus on the same volume and timbre for the entire minute. There is a perceptible sense of "opening up" in the middle and a clearer descent toward the end, which creates a coherent narrative arc.

**2. Which is more complete?**
**Clip Y** feels more like a complete composition. The variations in density and dynamics give it an "evolving" quality. While Clip X feels like an unvarying loop meant to be consumed in the background, Clip Y suggests a purposeful arrangement of sound. It provides the listener with a beginning, a middle shift, and an ending, which makes it feel less like a static sample and more like a structured piece.
