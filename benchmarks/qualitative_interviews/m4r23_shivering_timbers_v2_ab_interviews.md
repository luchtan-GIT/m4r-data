# M4R-23 — Blind Qualitative Evaluation of *Shivering Timbers v2 (Unpoliced)* & RMS-Matched A/B Showdown

> **Protocol:** Session A is a blind 3-turn open interview on `v2` alone (no mention of `v1`). Session B is a blind, RMS-matched side-by-side comparison (`Clip X` = `v2 Unpoliced`, `Clip Y` = `v1 Policed`).

## Session A — Blind Solo Interview on `v2 (Unpoliced)`

### Turn 1 — Whole Piece
**Listener:**
The track opens with a distinct, slightly detuned synth lead. Its timbre is crisp, but the deliberate instability creates a sense of unease or melancholy right from the first note.

The structure is linear and relies on the layering of textural elements rather than a traditional melodic progression. The introduction establishes a simple, rhythmic loop, which is then augmented by a metallic, high-frequency percussion element. This creates a good sense of spatial depth—the synth feels close and grounded, while the higher percussion adds a shimmer that expands the soundstage.

The turning point occurs around the midpoint when the track shifts from this initial rhythmic foundation into a more ambient phase. The decay of the synth sounds is handled well, especially in the latter half, where the lingering resonances create a sense of being in an empty, reverberant space.

However, the track feels somewhat unresolved. The tension built in the first 30 seconds doesn't lead to a substantial payoff or a clear thematic shift. By the end, the piece simply fades out rather than concluding. While the sound design is coherent, the lack of harmonic movement makes the 65-second duration feel a bit static; it works well as a background texture, but lacks the necessary variation to maintain engagement as a standalone focus piece.

### Turn 2 — Zone A vs. Zone B
**Listener:**
Comparing these two segments reinforces the impression of the track as a soundscape rather than a developing composition. 

In the first 18 seconds, the arrangement feels like it’s setting a stage—there’s a rhythmic pulse that creates an expectation of a specific groove or harmonic direction. It establishes a "character" with that distinct, detuned synth sound.

When the listening shifts to the middle section, it doesn't so much develop the ideas from the opening as it does move into a different register of the same theme. It feels like a reset because the rhythmic drive from the beginning dissolves, and the elements lose that sense of forward momentum. 

Instead of a natural evolution, it feels more like a change in perspective or a shift in the camera angle of the sound. The "tension" is static in both—neither clip creates a sense of arrival or departure. It remains in a state of suspended animation throughout both sections. If this were meant to be a narrative arc, the "middle" fails to build on the foundation laid in the "opening," leaving the piece feeling somewhat disjointed.

### Turn 3 — Zone C & Composer Note
**Listener:**
The ending is abrupt, not earned. Because the track doesn't build any narrative momentum or harmonic resolution, it doesn't really "conclude"—it just ceases to exist. It cuts off as if the listener simply reached the end of a time block rather than the end of a musical idea.

**The most honest note:**
*   **What worked best:** The sound design of the textures. There is a specific, gritty character to the synth sound that is genuinely interesting. It has a nice, tactile quality that makes the air in the mix feel slightly heavy and processed in a deliberate, aesthetic way.
*   **What needs work:** The structure. The track currently feels like a collection of textures that occur alongside one another rather than a composition that evolves. If you want a 65-second piece to feel "finished," you need to stop treating the track as a loop and start treating it as a story. Even in a minute, there needs to be a clear sense of tension (an arrival) and release (a departure), rather than just starting and stopping a sound machine.

## Session B — Blind RMS-Matched A/B Showdown (`Clip X = v2 Unpoliced`, `Clip Y = v1 Policed`)

### Blind A/B Listener 1 (`temp=0.7`)
This track relies on a repetitive, metallic percussive motif and a sustained, airy synth pad.

**Comparing the two clips:**

*   **Pacing and Tension:** Clip X uses a more aggressive, immediate compression. This forces the metallic high-end to the front, which increases the perceived tension but makes the track feel busier and slightly more claustrophobic. Clip Y takes a more relaxed approach to the dynamics. The percussive hits have more "air" and natural decay, which allows the synth pad to breathe and creates a more expansive soundstage.
*   **The Ending:** The ending in Clip X is abrupt and clipped. Because the compression is so heavy, the tail end of the final note is cut off prematurely, creating an artificial sensation of stopping. Clip Y allows the decay to resolve more naturally, providing a more satisfying and intentional conclusion.

**Evaluation:**

Clip Y is the more compelling listen. By allowing the transients and the natural decay of the sounds to remain intact, the piece establishes a better sense of space. Clip X feels over-processed, which creates a "forced" tension that fatigues the ear rather than engaging it. Clip Y feels more cohesive because it balances the percussive elements with the atmosphere of the synth pad, rather than letting the percussion dominate the mix.

### Blind A/B Listener 2 (`temp=0.95`)
In comparing these two clips, I hear the following:

**1. Comparison of Sound and Structure**

*   **Pacing and Tension:** Clip X uses more frequent, distinct dynamic shifts and a slightly more textured sound in the mid-range. This creates a push-pull effect, where the listener feels the tension build, release, and then build again. Clip Y feels more uniform. It maintains a steady momentum but misses the subtle ebbs and flows present in the first mix.
*   **Decay and Space:** Clip X seems to capture a better sense of spatial depth. The decay of the percussive elements sounds natural and allows the silence in the mix to have more weight. In Clip Y, the decay feels slightly compressed or restricted, which flattens the soundstage and makes the space feel more closed in.
*   **The Ending:** The ending of Clip X feels more deliberate; the fade aligns better with the natural decay of the instruments, providing a sense of completion. The end of Clip Y feels a bit abrupt, cutting off the resonance of the composition before it feels fully resolved.

**2. Overall Impression**

I find **Clip X** to be the more cohesive and compelling mix. 

Because Clip X respects the interplay between tension and release through its more nuanced dynamics and open spatial quality, it invites the listener into the soundscape. Clip Y, while technically proficient, lacks the textural variety that keeps the composition engaging over the full minute. The uniformity in the second clip makes it feel somewhat static, whereas Clip X breathes, making it feel less forced and more immersive.
